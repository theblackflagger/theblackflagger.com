Pirates don't fight for medals — they fight for freedom. That's the whole idea
behind The Black Flagger: small, sharp Mac apps made the way we'd want to use
them, and notes from the voyage along the way.

## What this blog is for

Short posts about the things we build and the decisions behind them:

- Why an app works the way it does
- What we learned shipping it
- The occasional deep dive into a detail nobody asked about

## What it isn't

No growth-hacking, no ten-step funnels. Just honest notes. If a post ever reads
like an ad, we got it wrong.

> Chase the work like buried treasure — until the thing in your hands is truly
> well-made.

## First up: BlackFlare

BlackFlare is out — a native macOS menu bar app for developers running
Claude Code and Codex.

It starts with the dumbest way to lose a night of work: you kick off a long
run, step away, and your Mac falls asleep. A sleeping Mac is a stalled agent.
BlackFlare keeps your Mac awake while a session runs, then lets it sleep
normally the moment the work is done.

And you don't have to babysit the terminal either. The instant a session
finishes — or needs your input — BlackFlare pings you: on your desktop, or
forwarded to Telegram or Slack so you can go touch some grass.

The menu bar is also mission control for your defaults — model, permission
mode, effort level — and it keeps your plan usage in plain sight, with a
heads-up before you cross a threshold you set. All of it on-device: no
accounts, no analytics, no telemetry.

The full story lives on its own site, [blackflare.dev](https://blackflare.dev)
— or jump straight to the [docs](https://blackflare.dev/docs).
